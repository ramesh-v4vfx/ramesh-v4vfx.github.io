Graphics are artistic and drawn live in code, not real images. The disk size follows the spin (a faster spin lets matter orbit closer), the colour follows the mass (lighter black holes run hotter and bluer), and the shape follows the type. In Compare, the two black holes are drawn true to scale against each other: each dark sphere is the event horizon, the solid ring is the photon sphere and the dashed ring is the ISCO.

Constants: c = 299,792,458 m/s · G = 6.6743 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² · M☉ = 1.988416 × 10³⁰ kg · R☉ = 695,700 km · 1 AU = 149,597,870.7 km. Schwarzschild radius = 2GM/c². With a measured spin, the black hole radius is the outer event horizon, GM/c² × (1 + √(1 − a²)). Light-crossing time is the Schwarzschild radius divided by c. The photon sphere is the closest circular orbit for light: 3GM/c² without spin, and 2GM/c² × [1 + cos(⅔ arccos(∓a))] with spin (minus for light moving with the spin). The ISCO is the innermost stable circular orbit for matter: 6GM/c² without spin, and the Bardeen et al. (1972) formula with spin. A negative spin is read as an orbit against the spin, and where spin is not measured (marked with *), no spin is assumed. The Hawking temperature is ħc³ / (8πGMk) without spin, multiplied by 2√(1−a²) / (1 + √(1−a²)) with spin (ħ = 1.0546 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, k = 1.3806 × 10⁻²³ J/K). Every black hole here is far colder than the 2.7 K cosmic microwave background, so each one takes in more heat than it gives off. Average density is the mass divided by the volume of a sphere with the Schwarzschild radius. Comoving distance is worked out from the light-travel distance with a flat ΛCDM model (H₀ = 67.66 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.3111) and is shown only for objects beyond 10 million light-years.
