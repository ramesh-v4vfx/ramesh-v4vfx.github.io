ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL — the mass boundaries and remnant masses below follow standard stellar-evolution rules of thumb, and the exact limits are uncertain. At solar metallicity: ~0.25 M☉ blue-dwarf/red-giant split among red dwarfs, ~0.5 M☉ red dwarf/white dwarf, ~8 M☉ white dwarf/supernova threshold, ~20 M☉ neutron star/black hole threshold, ~40 M☉ failed-supernova threshold. Metallicity (0.01–3 Z☉; above ~2 Z☉ is extrapolated) shifts these slightly and moves the pair-instability window much more: at very low metallicity there is pulsational pair-instability from ~80 M☉, a pair-instability supernova with no remnant from ~130 to ~250 M☉, and direct collapse above that; as metallicity rises, winds strip the star and that window moves up and out of range, so black holes get lighter (wind-limited near ~20 M☉ at solar). White dwarf masses use a linear initial–final mass relation (≈0.109·M + 0.394, nudged by metallicity), sizes use the Nauenberg mass–radius relation, and red-dwarf life uses a steep power law anchored near 10 trillion years at 0.1 M☉; above 0.5 M☉ main-sequence life uses ~10 Gyr × M−2.5 with a floor near 3 Myr, scaled weakly with metallicity. Real outcomes also depend on binarity, rotation and fallback physics not modeled here. Metals strengthen stellar winds: a metal-rich star sheds more mass before it dies, so it leaves a lighter remnant. Preset-star metallicities are published values where available: Sirius A ≈ 0.85 Z☉ (bulk composition from Hubble modelling; its surface iron reads higher), Betelgeuse ≈ 0.86 Z☉ ([Fe/H] ≈ −0.06, 2025 red-supergiant survey), R136a1 ≈ 0.4 Z☉ (typical of the Large Magellanic Cloud), Proxima ≈ 1 Z☉ as a midpoint of values that range from roughly 0.3 to 2, and Antares assumed solar because no good measurement was found. Collapse/explosion timing is dramatized; the real timescale of each stage is shown under the preview.
