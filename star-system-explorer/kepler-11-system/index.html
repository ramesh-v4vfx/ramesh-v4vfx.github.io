ABOUT THIS SYSTEM

Kepler-11: a packed, flat system

  • ~2,100 lyDISTANCE
  • 6CONFIRMED PLANETS
  • 2011ANNOUNCED
  • TransitDISCOVERY METHOD

Kepler-11 is a Sun-like star in the constellation Cygnus. NASA's Kepler space telescope found six planets around it by watching for tiny dips in its light as each planet passed in front. The discovery was announced in 2011, and it was the first known system with six transiting planets.

Five of the planets (b to f) orbit closer to the star than Mercury does to our Sun. Their years last from about 10 to 47 days. The sixth, Kepler-11 g, orbits a little farther out than Mercury. Their masses are low for their size, which suggests the bigger ones are wrapped in thick envelopes of light gas rather than being solid rock.

The orbits lie almost exactly in one plane, seen almost edge-on from Earth, which is why we can see all six transits. Use EDGE-ON to see the system the way Kepler did, and SOLAR SYSTEM to lay Mercury, Venus and Earth's orbits over it. Everything is drawn to true scale by default, so the planets are tiny next to their orbits: tap one in the list to fly to it. Switch SCALE to STYLIZED for a spread-out view with enlarged bodies.
